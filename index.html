Washington, D.C. • 1917

The Committee on Public Information

When the United States entered World War I, President Woodrow Wilson created the Committee on Public Information (CPI) .

Its job was to build support for the war and convince millions of Americans that supporting the war effort was important.

George Creel A journalist chosen to lead the CPI. Creel organized a massive campaign to influence American public opinion.
How the CPI Reached People Posters, newspaper material, pamphlets, films, advertising, and speeches by the Four Minute Men.
What Is Propaganda? Communication designed to influence what people think, feel, or do.
Important This activity includes both American and British propaganda. Allied nations often used similar techniques.

COMMITTEE ON PUBLIC INFORMATION

George Creel

Chairman • 1917–1919

The CPI did not fight with guns.

It fought for public opinion.

Creel organized writers, artists, advertisers, filmmakers, and public speakers to encourage Americans to support the war.

Posters were especially powerful because an image and a few words could quickly create fear, pride, guilt, anger, or patriotism.
