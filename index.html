Washington, D.C. • 1917

The Committee on Public Information

When the United States entered World War I, President Woodrow Wilson created the Committee on Public Information (CPI) to build public support for the war.

Wilson selected journalist George Creel to lead it. Creel coordinated writers, artists, advertisers, filmmakers, and speakers to persuade Americans to support mobilization.

George Creel Chairman of the CPI. He believed public opinion could be mobilized through information and persuasion.
How Americans Were Reached Posters, pamphlets, newspaper material, films, advertising, and speeches by the Four Minute Men.
What Is Propaganda? Communication designed to influence what people think, feel, or do.
Important Distinction The CPI was central to wartime persuasion, but not every poster in this activity was made by the CPI. Other U.S. agencies and organizations also produced propaganda.

Best Streak

American World War I propaganda asked citizens to enlist, buy bonds, conserve resources, support home-front organizations, and identify themselves with the nation. The posters often used fear, pride, guilt, sacrifice, and patriotic symbols to make those messages powerful.
